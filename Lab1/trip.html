An Unforgettable Journey

Day 1: Arrival in Paradise
The moment I stepped off the plane, I was greeted by the warm Caribbean air and the scent of salt and flowers. The drive to the hotel was breathtaking—palm trees lining the roads, the ocean sparkling under the sun. After settling in, I took a walk along La Caravelle Beach, feeling the soft white sand beneath my feet. The sunset painted the sky in shades of orange and pink.
Day 2: Exploring Basse-Terre
Today was all about nature! I hiked up La Soufrière, an active volcano covered in lush rainforest. The trek was tough, but the view from the top was absolutely worth it. Later, I cooled off under the waterfalls of Carbet Falls—the sound of rushing water mixed with birdsong made it feel like a dream.
Day 3: Discovering Local Culture
I wandered through the markets of Pointe-à-Pitre, where the air was filled with the rich aroma of spices, coffee, and tropical fruits. I tried bokit, a delicious fried sandwich stuffed with meat and vegetables. In the evening, I stumbled upon a live Gwo Ka performance—traditional Guadeloupean drumming and dancing. The energy was contagious, and I even joined in!
Day 4: Sailing to Îles des Saintes
Took a boat to Îles des Saintes, a group of stunning little islands with turquoise waters. I snorkeled among colorful fish and coral reefs, then relaxed on Pain de Sucre Beach. The seafood here is incredible—grilled lobster with Creole spices was the perfect meal to end the day.
Day 5: A Farewell to Remember
For my last day, I visited Grand Anse Beach, one of the most beautiful spots I’ve ever seen. I let the waves wash over my feet and just took in the moment. Guadeloupe is more than a destination—it’s an experience, full of warmth, rhythm, and unforgettable beauty. I’ll be back someday.

Local Music

It all started one rainy afternoon when I was wandering around the city, trying to escape the monotony of my routine. I had no particular destination in mind, just a need for something new. As I walked through a quiet street, I noticed the sound of live music drifting from a small, cozy café tucked in the corner. The soft melody intrigued me, so I stepped inside to find a local musician playing acoustic guitar. The atmosphere was warm, with a gentle crowd listening intently. I grabbed a seat and soon found myself lost in the rhythms and lyrics of songs I had never heard before. The music felt different from the mainstream stuff I was used to – raw, real, and full of emotion.

Curious, I started chatting with the barista about the musician. She told me that he was part of a local scene, where artists regularly performed in small venues like this, sharing their work with anyone who would listen. She also mentioned that there were music events happening all over the city, but they were often overlooked by tourists.

Intrigued by this hidden world of local talent, I began exploring more. I started attending open mics, listening to street performers, and visiting cafes where live music was the heartbeat of the place. Slowly, I learned about local bands, genres, and sounds that were unique to the area. I realized that music wasn’t just something you heard on the radio—it was a living, breathing part of the community. That rainy afternoon marked the beginning of my love for local music, and now, it’s something I cherish and seek out wherever I go.

Did you know?

Guadeloupe celebrates one of the most vibrant and colorful Carnival festivals in the Caribbean, typically from January to March. The festival is filled with parades, costumes, music, and dancing, showcasing the island's African and French heritage.

Guadeloupe’s food is a fusion of French, African, and Caribbean flavors. Local dishes include accras (fried fritters), colombo (a curry dish), and bokit (a type of fried sandwich). Fresh seafood and tropical fruits are also abundant.
